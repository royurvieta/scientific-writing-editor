# Anti-slop patterns for scientific English and Spanish

Use these signals to edit or audit. They identify possible problems, not forbidden words. Scientific fidelity and the author's voice always take priority. For positive voice decisions, use [personal-voice.md](personal-voice.md).

## English patterns

### Empty openings

Signals: `It is worth noting`, `It is important to highlight`, `The key point is`, or a prepared question before the claim.

Action: begin with the fact when the opening adds no logic, scope, or necessary courtesy.

### Decorative contrast and fragments

Signals: `It is not X. It is Y.`, stacked fragments, colon reveals, or `That is the whole point`.

Action: state the conclusion normally. Keep contrast when it protects a real distinction such as association versus causation.

### Vague interpretation

Signals: clauses with `highlighting`, `underscoring`, `reflecting`, or `showcasing` that add no supported consequence.

Action: remove the clause or state the exact supported consequence. Keep these verbs when they describe a real operation or interpretation already present in the source.

### Inflated importance

Signals: `plays a crucial role`, `provides valuable insight`, `robust and comprehensive`, `multifaceted`, `significant contribution`, or `marks a pivotal moment`.

Action: present the result, comparison, or bounded implication. Keep `significant`, `robust`, or `important` when each has a precise scientific function.

### Vague attribution

Signals: `studies show`, `experts agree`, or `it is widely accepted` without an identifiable source.

Action: name the available source, remove the attribution, or flag the missing support. Never invent a citation.

### Forced synonymy and academic substitutes

Signals: cycling among technical terms as if they were equivalent; using `employed`, `utilized`, `facilitates`, or `leverages` when a simpler verb is equally exact.

Action: repeat the correct technical term and use the direct verb.

### Robotic structure and decorative endings

Signals: identical paragraph shapes, repeated mini-summaries, excessive balance, metaphorical endings, or a final sentence designed mainly to sound quotable.

Action: end at the last supported fact, limitation, conclusion, or action.

## Equivalent Spanish patterns

- Empty openings: `cabe destacar`, `vale la pena señalar`, `es importante mencionar`.
- Inflated importance: `constituye un hito`, `resulta fundamental`, `pone en valor`.
- Vague interpretation: `evidencia`, `refleja`, `permite apreciar`, `invita a pensar` without concrete support.
- Vague attribution: `los estudios demuestran`, `los expertos coinciden` without a source.
- Decorative contrast: `no se trata de X, sino de Y` when stating Y is enough.
- Inflated formulas: `a fin de`, `con el objetivo de`, `en el marco de` when a direct verb carries the same meaning.
- Forced synonymy, empty adjectives, repeated conclusions, and mechanically symmetrical paragraphs.

Keep any expression that performs a necessary scientific or logical function. Removing a pattern does not authorize a stronger claim, a new implication, or a change in register.

## Formatting signals

Decorative bold, emoji headings, unnecessary micro-sections, and automatic em dashes can make prose feel generated. Change formatting only when it improves navigation or meaning. An em dash is allowed when it is clearer than a comma, period, or parenthesis.

## Audit boundary

In DETECT mode, report observable patterns with short quotations and contextual explanations. Do not infer authorship, assign a probability, rewrite without permission, or manufacture faults in clear prose.
