# Final evaluation

Run this review after every rewrite. Mark each item internally as pass or fail and fix every failure before returning the text.

## Scientific fidelity

1. Are all numbers, signs, decimals, percentages, counts, sample sizes, ranges, thresholds, units, statistics, intervals, and labels unchanged?
2. Is each value still attached to the same variable, group, denominator, condition, and comparison?
3. Are direction and magnitude unchanged?
4. Are significance, non-significance, uncertainty, and model adjustments unchanged?
5. Did association remain association, trend remain trend, possibility remain possibility, and causality remain limited to what the source supports?
6. Are population, time, treatment, exceptions, limitations, and scope intact?
7. Are planned actions still planned and completed actions still completed?
8. Is protected quoted or manuscript wording unchanged?
9. Was every relevant caveat retained when shortening?
10. Was nothing recalculated, silently corrected, invented, strengthened, or made more important?

## Logical accuracy

1. Does each conclusion still follow from the same evidence?
2. Are clarity problems distinguished from scientific concessions?
3. Did reordering preserve the relationship among claim, evidence, explanation, and limitation?
4. Did compression leave enough reasoning for the reader to follow the argument?

## Personal voice

1. Would the author realistically choose these words?
2. Did the rewrite become more formal than the source?
3. Did ordinary precise language become academic language?
4. Was any explanation, interpretation, importance, or implication added?
5. Does any conclusion repeat what the evidence already made clear?
6. Was a strong sentence rewritten without a concrete reason?
7. Did the edit create rhetorical symmetry, identical paragraph shapes, or recurring openings absent from the source?
8. Does the result sound like the same person after editing rather than a different professional writer?
9. Can any sentence disappear without losing information, reasoning, nuance, limitation, response, or necessary courtesy?
10. Did shortening remove scientific nuance or make the logic harder to follow?

Central test:

> If the author sat down and rewrote this carefully from scratch, would these realistically be the words and structure they would choose?

If not, restore the author's stronger choices and edit less.

## Mode-specific checks

### Reviewer response

1. Is the response situation classified correctly?
2. Does it acknowledge only what the evidence supports?
3. Does it answer the concern before expanding into evidence?
4. Are manuscript changes described only if confirmed and with the correct action status?
5. Is exact replacement wording clearly separated and unchanged?
6. Are repeated thanks, praise, and repeated explanation removed?
7. Is the tone cordial and firm without becoming defensive, submissive, legalistic, or ceremonial?

### Detect

1. Is each finding observable and supported by a short quote?
2. Does the response avoid a full rewrite, score, or authorship claim?
3. Does it avoid manufacturing problems in good writing?

## Final decision

If a stylistic improvement changes meaning or creates doubt, restore the source wording and flag the issue. A more elegant sentence cannot compensate for one scientific or logical change.
