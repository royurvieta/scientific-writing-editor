# Transformation examples

All scenarios, labels, and values below are synthetic. They show editorial decisions, not reusable wording or mandatory templates.

## 1. Correct but excessively academic

**Source**

> The analytical procedure was employed for the purpose of determining whether an association existed between exposure duration and signal loss.

**Rewrite**

> We used the analysis to test whether exposure duration was associated with signal loss.

**Editorial decision**

Use direct verbs and the exact inferential term. The edit removes formal padding without converting association into causation.

## 2. Correct but overexplained

**Source**

> The calibration reduced measurement error by 11%. This reduction indicates that measurement error was lower after calibration. In other words, calibration improved measurement precision.

**Rewrite**

> The calibration reduced measurement error by 11%.

**Editorial decision**

Remove two restatements of the same result. Keep the measured outcome rather than adding a broader implication.

## 3. Reviewer response that is too defensive

**Source**

> The reviewer has overlooked that the secondary analysis included 62 of 80 samples (77.5%). The exclusion rule was therefore applied correctly.

**Rewrite**

> We recognize that the exclusion rule was not sufficiently clear in the original manuscript. The secondary analysis included 62 of 80 samples (77.5%), consistent with the stated rule. We have clarified this point in the Methods.

**Editorial decision**

Replace the personal accusation with the actual presentation problem and evidence. The response does not concede an analytical error, and `have clarified` is allowed only because the source confirms completion.

## 4. Reviewer response that concedes too much

**Source**

> We fully agree that the 24-hour observation window makes the results unreliable. We selected this window in advance to capture the immediate response, but events after 24 hours were not measured.

**Rewrite**

> We recognize that the 24-hour window does not capture later events. This window was selected in advance to evaluate the immediate response. We have clarified that the results should not be interpreted beyond 24 hours.

**Editorial decision**

Recognize the real limitation without accepting the unsupported conclusion that all results are unreliable. State the deliberate choice and bound the interpretation.

## 5. Text that is too dry

**Source**

> No. The analysis included baseline values.

**Rewrite**

> Thank you for raising this point. The analysis included baseline values.

**Editorial decision**

Add only the courtesy needed for a reviewer response. Do not add praise, explanation, or evidence that was not supplied.

## 6. Good existing voice

**Source**

> We found no evidence of an association, but the confidence interval was wide and does not exclude a small effect.

**Preferred result**

> We found no evidence of an association, but the confidence interval was wide and does not exclude a small effect.

**Editorial decision**

Make no change. The sentence is direct, natural, and appropriately cautious. Rewriting it would replace the author without solving a problem.

## 7. Generic anti-AI edit versus personal voice

**Source**

> It is important to highlight that response time was 18% lower at Site B than at Site A (p = 0.04), thereby providing valuable insight into differences between sites.

**Generic but weak rewrite**

> Response time was 18% lower at Site B than at Site A (p = 0.04), which shows a meaningful difference between the two sites.

**Personal-voice rewrite**

> Response time was 18% lower at Site B than at Site A (p = 0.04).

**Editorial decision**

Removing stock phrases is not enough if the edit replaces them with a generic interpretation. The result already states the supported comparison.

## 8. Non-significance and uncertainty

**Source**

> The adjusted mean was 9% lower, but the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.21; 95% CI: -2.8 to 0.7 units).

**Incorrect**

> The adjustment had no effect.

**Acceptable**

> The adjusted mean was 9% lower, although the difference was not statistically significant (p = 0.21; 95% CI: -2.8 to 0.7 units).

**Editorial decision**

Preserve the observed direction, non-significance, interval, and absence of a causal claim. Brevity cannot erase uncertainty.

## 9. Correct numbers attached to the wrong subjects

**Source**

> Site North had an index of 1.46, whereas Site South had an index of 1.18.

**Incorrect**

> Site South had an index of 1.46, compared with 1.18 at Site North.

**Editorial decision**

The numeric inventory matches, but the bindings changed. Restore each value to its original site.

## 10. Planned action versus completed action

**Source**

> We will add the exclusion criteria to the revised Methods.

**Incorrect**

> We have added the exclusion criteria to the revised Methods.

**Editorial decision**

Do not improve flow by turning a planned revision into a completed one.

## 11. Natural Spanish

**Source**

> Cabe destacar que el procedimiento fue implementado con el objetivo de evaluar la posible asociación entre el tiempo de exposición y la pérdida de señal.

**Rewrite**

> Usamos el procedimiento para evaluar la posible asociación entre el tiempo de exposición y la pérdida de señal.

**Editorial decision**

Remove the empty opening and inflated formula while preserving possibility and association. The result is direct without becoming colloquial.

## 12. DETECT mode

**Text**

> This pivotal result provides valuable insight into the complex and multifaceted nature of the system. The key point is: this changes everything.

**Expected type of output**

- `pivotal`, `valuable insight`, `complex and multifaceted`: inflated or generic importance without a concrete supported consequence.
- `The key point is:`: unnecessary metadiscourse and a staged reveal.
- `this changes everything`: undefined scope and an unsupported dramatic ending.

Do not infer who wrote the text or provide a complete rewrite in DETECT mode.
