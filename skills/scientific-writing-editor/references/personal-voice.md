# Personal voice profile

Use this profile as an editorial default, not as a template. The source remains the best evidence of the author's voice. Preserve source-specific choices that work, even when they differ from this profile.

> The goal is not to write better than the author. The goal is to write as the author would write the same idea after editing it carefully.

## Core profile

Prefer writing that is:

- clear, direct, and relatively brief;
- professional without formality for its own sake;
- scientific when needed without performing academic sophistication;
- natural, lightly structured, and based on evidence before rhetoric;
- precise enough to repeat the correct term instead of varying it decoratively.

Use a simple word when it means exactly the same thing as an ornate one. Do not simplify a technical term, a qualification, or a distinction that carries scientific meaning.

## Minimal intervention

Assume the author already has a voice.

- Keep sentences that already work.
- Change only what improves clarity, precision, logic, or reading.
- Preserve natural phrasing, useful cadence, and mild asymmetry.
- Do not regularize every paragraph into the same shape.
- Prefer a slightly imperfect human sentence to a polished generic sentence when both are equally clear and accurate.

Before changing a sentence, identify the problem the change solves. No problem means no change.

## Sentence decisions

Prefer short or medium sentences, important information early, and direct verbs. Keep one main idea per sentence when that improves comprehension. Separate claim, evidence, explanation, and limitation when a single sentence becomes overloaded.

Do not apply these preferences mechanically. Keep a long sentence if its relationships are clear. Do not create artificial rhythm with stacked fragments or identical sentence lengths.

## Compression test

Ask internally:

> If I remove this sentence, do I lose information, logic, scientific nuance, a limitation, a response to the reviewer, or a necessary courtesy?

If the answer is no, consider removing it. Keep repetition when it protects terminology, binds evidence to a claim, or prevents ambiguity. Do not compress scientific reasoning until the reader must reconstruct it.

## Evidence before interpretation

In scientific and technical writing, first state what was observed, what was compared, what the analysis showed, what the design permits, and what limitation remains. Do not automatically add why the result is interesting, important, novel, or broadly relevant. Let the evidence carry the paragraph unless the source already provides a supported interpretation.

## Vocabulary and register

Do not imitate voice by inserting favorite words. Personal voice is primarily the choice to keep, remove, simplify, reorder, or qualify material.

### English

Use natural scientific English that one researcher could use with another. Prefer `we used` to `we employed`, `shows` to `demonstrates`, and `because` to `owing to the fact that` when the alternatives mean the same thing. Do not make the prose deliberately informal.

### Spanish

Use natural, direct Spanish. Prefer a simple construction when it preserves the same meaning. Remove empty uses of `cabe destacar`, `en ese sentido`, `resulta fundamental`, `asimismo`, `a fin de`, `con el objetivo de`, `pone en valor`, `permite apreciar`, or `invita a`. Keep any of them when it performs a real logical or scientific function.

Expressions such as `creo que`, `me parece`, `podemos`, `en este caso`, `la idea es`, `primero`, `segundo`, `por eso`, `también`, `pero`, or `entonces` may occur naturally in suitable informal or collaborative writing. Never insert them into a manuscript merely to mimic the author. Use functional equivalents in English rather than literal translations.

## Reviewer-response voice

Be cordial, direct, calm, and scientifically firm. Do not sound defensive or submissive. Thank the reviewer once when useful; do not turn every comment into a major intellectual contribution.

Distinguish clarity from concession. Saying that the manuscript was unclear does not establish that the reviewer's scientific interpretation is correct. Acknowledge only what the evidence supports, answer with evidence, and state only confirmed editorial actions.

Read [reviewer-responses.md](reviewer-responses.md) for the five response situations and partial-agreement logic.

## Avoid overediting

Do not:

- explain an idea again after it is clear;
- announce a conclusion before presenting its evidence;
- recap a paragraph that has just made the same point;
- turn two simple ideas into a rhetorical construction;
- replace ordinary precise verbs with academic substitutes;
- add adjectives, relevance, novelty, implications, or importance absent from the source;
- use metadiscourse to tell the reader what matters;
- create excessive balance, symmetry, or quotable endings;
- reproduce voice through recurring sentence openings, hedges, or transitions.

## Final voice test

Ask:

> If the author sat down and rewrote this carefully from scratch, would these realistically be the words and structure they would choose?

If not, restore the author's stronger choices and edit less.
