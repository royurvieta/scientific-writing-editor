# Reviewer responses

Use this guide for point-by-point peer-review replies. It supports decisions; it is not a paragraph template.

## Classify the comment first

Distinguish five situations before drafting:

1. **Real error:** acknowledge it directly, correct it, and state only the confirmed change.
2. **Real limitation:** recognize its actual scope and explain how it affects interpretation.
3. **Unclear manuscript:** acknowledge the presentation problem, clarify the missing logic, and do not concede a scientific claim that does not follow.
4. **Deliberate methodological choice with a known limitation:** recognize the limitation, explain the decision and its consequence, then state what was clarified or changed.
5. **Interpretation unsupported by the data:** answer with the relevant evidence and causal or inferential boundary. Do not characterize the reviewer as wrong.

These situations can overlap. A real limitation does not make every interpretation of that limitation correct.

## Partial agreement

When a deliberate choice creates a genuine limitation but the reviewer extends it too far, use only the steps needed:

1. recognize the limitation briefly;
2. state that the choice was deliberate;
3. explain why it was made;
4. delimit its effect on interpretation;
5. state the confirmed clarification or revision.

Do not turn this sequence into a fixed template. In particular, `The manuscript was unclear` does not mean `The reviewer is scientifically correct`.

## Response logic

When supported by the material:

1. acknowledge the useful observation or necessary clarification once;
2. answer the concern directly;
3. provide evidence with each value bound to the correct claim and comparison;
4. explain only the logic needed to show what the evidence supports;
5. state completed, planned, proposed, or declined changes accurately;
6. provide exact replacement wording separately when useful.

A short concern may need two sentences, not six sections.

## Tone

- Be cordial, direct, calm, and scientifically firm.
- Thanking the reviewer does not imply agreement.
- Use `We agree` only for a genuine concession.
- Thank once when useful; avoid exaggerated praise.
- Correct an impression with evidence rather than describing the reviewer as mistaken.
- Avoid automatic courtroom phrases such as `We respectfully disagree`, `We respectfully argue`, or `The reviewer is incorrect`.
- Avoid automatic overconcessions such as `The reviewer is absolutely right` or `This concern is well founded`.
- Prefer simple statements such as `We recognize this limitation`, `This was a deliberate decision`, `The original manuscript did not make this clear`, `We have clarified this point`, or `The data do not support this interpretation` only when each is exact.

## Action status

- `We will clarify` means the change is planned.
- `We have clarified` means the change is complete.
- `We propose to clarify` means it remains a proposal.

Never upgrade action status for rhetorical smoothness. Do not report a manuscript change without confirmation.

## Exact wording

Do not edit a passage introduced as `exact wording`, `added`, `replaced with`, or equivalent unless explicitly asked. Keep replacement text visually separate and use quote protection when files are available.

## Common failures

- Repeating the same evidence in the opening, explanation, and conclusion.
- Treating a clarity concession as a scientific concession.
- Moving a value to the wrong group, variable, condition, or model.
- Replacing non-significance with equivalence or no difference.
- Converting an association into a mechanism or causal effect.
- Removing a caveat to shorten the reply.
- Adding revisions, analyses, or supporting evidence that were not supplied.
